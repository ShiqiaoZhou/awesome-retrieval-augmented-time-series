# Chronological ordering and date sources

Updated 15 October 2026. These rules change presentation, not the 127-record corpus, scope decisions or original first-public fields.

1. **Newest first, with year headings only.** Each paper is one linked-title line followed by its venue in parentheses. There are no separate venue headings or preprint blocks.
2. **Conferences:** use the official full-event start date. A main conference, its workshops, Findings and associated tracks share one parent-event date and stay together. Labels distinguish the workshop/track. This date is not the paper's presentation day or proceedings-publication date. Conference acceptance-notification dates do not order this view.
3. **Journals:** prefer verified first-online publication, including an explicitly published accepted-author version when that is the publisher's earliest publication. Otherwise use an evidenced issue month/year. A later issue, metadata deposit, site migration or updated webpage is not silently substituted for a known earlier publication. Source-specific caveats are retained below and in the date register.
4. **Preprints:** interleave by earliest verified initial posting, rather than the latest revision. If an earlier submission exists but its posting date is unknown, use the earliest verified date and say so. A record without a verified journal/conference is displayed as preprint; this label does not itself verify manuscript availability or acceptance.
5. **Precision:** dates retain day, month or year precision. A month-only record follows day-dated records in that month; a year-only record follows dated records in that year. This deterministic fallback does not claim exact within-interval chronology. Completely unknown years appear last. Equal dates are grouped by parent conference, then titles; main entries precede associated workshops within a group. No missing month or day is invented.
6. **Future events:** CIKM 2026 and NeurIPS 2026 were scheduled after the 15 October cutoff. Their verified accepted/program-listed papers are placed at those scheduled event dates. This does not assert that the events have occurred.
7. Stable IDs, original dates, provenance and inclusion decisions remain in the reconciled source files. Current display-date evidence is an additive layer. ORCA and ForecastCompass receive evidence-backed NeurIPS 2026 display labels, without changing their scope classifications. No individual acceptance date is inferred.

## Journal and preprint dates

Every displayed record has its exact date string, precision, date type and source in [publication-dates.json](../data/publication-dates.json). The [display-order data](../data/display-order.json) preserves the sorted IDs, original first-public fields, scope labels and new venue evidence. [venue-order.json](../data/venue-order.json) contains the event register.

Only the precision supported by the source is used. Some older journal records retain issue-month/year fallbacks; repository-only or inaccessible submission records can retain year-only or unknown dates. Publisher histories, official programmes, arXiv version histories and published-article date statements are used rather than DOI/arXiv identifier guesses. The evidence caveats identify mirrored original articles and bibliographic metadata fallbacks.
